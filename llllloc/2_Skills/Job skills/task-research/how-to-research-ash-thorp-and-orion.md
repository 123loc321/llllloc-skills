# Ash Thorp and ORION — what is checked and what is not

Question: llllloc names ORION (altcinc.com/work/orion) and "the style of that artist" as the
target look. What is actually true about it?
Checked 7 September 2026. Two searches plus two page fetches.

## THE FACTS — CONFIRMED

- ORION, 2023. Director and creator: **ASH THORP**. Co-produced with **Hydra Studios** under
  **ALT Creative Inc.** Source: the ALT project page's own credit list.
- Music by Aeph and Pilotpriest. CG supervisor Sergio Caires. Look development supervisor
  John Barclay. Environments Sergio Caires and Rodrigo Henrique. Grade and edit by Thorp.
- CG animation, not live action.
- **THE METHOD, and it is the useful part.** Thorp made **125 chronological narrative
  paintings** first, over months, before any animation. In his words each one "represents a
  still from the film", and they were "the foundational blueprint for the film."
- His stated intent: "Each shot and setup relies on traditional art fundamentals in an attempt
  to get your mind to fall further into the world." He calls it "a contemplative moving piece
  of art", about "sacrifice and violence explored through the lens of fantasy and myth."

## VERIFIED 7 SEPTEMBER 2026 - HE SENT THE FILM, IT WAS WATCHED

He supplied the film after being asked for one frame. 2 minutes 15 seconds, 24 fps, 1920x1080
letterboxed to 2.39:1. Sixteen frames pulled across its length and read. Everything below is
measured off those frames, not from an article.

- **FOG IS THE ONLY MODELING TOOL.** No hard key, no rim light, no cast shadow anywhere in the
  film. Figures separate from the ground by VALUE against atmosphere and by nothing else.
- **VALUE RUNS TO TWO POLES WITH NO MIDDLE.** Either a near-black figure on a lit fog field, or
  a dark figure on a near-white fog field. Almost nothing sits at mid grey.
- **ONE TEMPERATURE PER SHOT**, at very low saturation. Three families across the film: cool
  teal, pale lavender, and one amber sequence. Shots do not mix two.
- **NO SMALL BRIGHT ACCENT.** The accent is always a LARGE SOFT GLOW - a practical bloomed
  through fog, or smoke lit from behind. There is no hot pixel anywhere.
- **ICON STAGING.** Dead center, symmetrical, frontal or straight from behind. No three-quarter
  hero angles. The mask at 37s, the gate at 29s and the two figures at 46s are all centered.
- **THE FIGURE IS SMALL**, often a third of the frame height or less, in a wide empty field. The
  scale of the emptiness carries the emotion, not the creature.
- **DETAIL IS SUPPRESSED ON PURPOSE.** The armor is barely readable. The silhouette carries the
  shot. Material craft shows in the close inserts, never in the wides.
- **TYPE:** white serif, small, centered, wide tracking, on pure black.

**THE LESSON FOR THE WORKFLOW.** Before the film arrived, this note correctly refused to
describe the look and asked for one frame. He sent the whole film in one message. Asking for the
picture cost one turn and bought eight measured facts. Never guess a named work; ask.

## WHAT WAS NOT VERIFIABLE FROM THE PAGES ALONE, BEFORE THE FILM

The concrete LOOK. The grade, the palette in numbers, the lens language, the material register,
the atmosphere. Two sources were fetched and BOTH refused to describe it: the ALT page carries
credits and intent only, and the trade write-up says outright that it gives no technical detail.

So nothing about ORION's color or light goes into a prompt, a deck or a client sentence from
memory. This is the Akira failure waiting to happen on a new name.

**THE CHEAPEST FIX: ask him for ONE frame grab.** He watched it. A single still settles the
grade, the lens and the material register in one look, and costs him one drag-and-drop. His own
rule already says a visual argument is settled by one picture, not by more words.

## THE ONE THING THAT TRANSFERS WITHOUT SEEING A FRAME

The 125-paintings method. It says the look was DESIGNED as stills, in paint, on fundamentals,
before anything moved. That is a workflow claim, not a style claim, and it is checkable.

For llllloc it maps exactly onto his own library: paint the key frames first, on
`value-and-light/backlight-and-separation.md`, `color/color-as-a-system.md` and
`composition/what-the-eye-reads-first.md`, and only then build or generate. "Like ORION"
therefore means "key frames first, fundamentals under every one", not a filter.
