# How to research a named work or artist before naming their style — method note
Asked: llllloc names a film, a book or an artist as the look. What do I have to check first?
Researched: 5 September 2026 · Re-check after: 4 November 2026
Tool and version: craft note. Applies to any model and any deliverable.

## THE RULE

A NAMED WORK IS A FACT QUESTION, NOT A STYLE WORD.

"like in Akira", "Ghibli", "Fortiche", "Blade Runner", "Moebius" all look like style
adjectives. They are not. They are claims about a real thing that exists, and writing what
it looks like from memory produces confident, wrong prompts that reach his drive.

Check three things before the name goes in a prompt:
1. THE LINE. How is it actually drawn? Weight, hatching, where solid black goes.
2. THE COLOR. Flat or graded? Who colored it, and with what?
3. THE ONE THING EVERYONE GETS WRONG about it.

Two searches. One on the drawing, one on the color.

## THE WORKED CASE — AKIRA, 5 SEPTEMBER 2026

A prompt shipped saying "hard mechanical ink register of late-1980s Otomo-era seinen
manga", plus "heavy black spotting", "solid black wedges" and "flat color". Written from
memory. Three of those were wrong.

What the sources actually say:

- OTOMO USES ALMOST NO SOLID BLACK. Analysis of the Akira artwork finds solid black used
  mainly in the hair, and value built from line density instead. The prompt had asked for
  heavy black masses.
- THERE IS NO CROSSHATCHING. The same analysis finds essentially no intersecting shading
  lines. Tone comes from the weight, the length and the frequency of parallel strokes.
  The prompt had asked for hatching without saying it must never cross.
- THE COLOR IS NOT FLAT. The color Akira manga was computer-colored by Steve Oliff at
  Olyoptics, and the thing that made it new was gradation, in his words the ability to put
  gradations anywhere at all. The prompt had asked for flat fills, which is the opposite.
- Otomo also varies the LINE BY MATERIAL: a rounded contour on figures, ruled straight
  lines on architecture and hard kit, a broken sketchy line on soft things.

Confidence: the line findings are LIKELY, from one strong art analysis. The Oliff gradation
finding is CONFIRMED from the colorist's own account. The palette philosophy is UNVERIFIED,
so do not state one.

## WHAT CHANGED MY OUTPUT

The whole render block of the prompt inverted. Solid black went from "heavy wedges" to
"hair only". Hatching gained "and the strokes never cross". Flat fills became airbrushed
gradations under the ink, two or three steps per part.

## WHAT DOES NOT WORK

- Naming an era instead of checking a work. "Late-1980s Otomo-era ink" sounds researched
  and carries no fact.
- Trusting that a famous look is the look you remember. The famous thing about Akira's
  color is that it was NOT flat, which is the opposite of the manga default.

## SOURCES

- Scott McDaniel, art analysis of an Akira page, scottmcd.net — minimal solid black, no
  intersecting shading lines, value from line density.
- Colouring the Akira Manga, akira2019.com — Steve Oliff and Olyoptics, computer color,
  gradations anywhere.
- Fun Japan, guide to Japanese school uniforms — sailor collar and ribbon, gakuran standing
  collar with emblem buttons, blazer with chest emblem and pleated check skirt.
