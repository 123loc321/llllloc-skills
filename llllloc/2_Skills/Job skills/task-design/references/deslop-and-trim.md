# THE DESLOP PASS AND THE FAT TRIM

Part of `llllloc:task-design`. "RULE 11" and "RULE 12" in any rule point here. Both run before handoff, the trim first.

## RULE 11 — THE DESLOP PASS. Runs last, on everything.

AI design has tells. They are not ugly. They are DEFAULT, the move a model reaches for when nobody decided. None is a ban, and the brief can earn any of them. Ask of each tell found: was this DECIDED or DEFAULT? If I remove it, does the piece get worse? If not, it goes.

- STRUCTURE: same-size cards of icon plus heading plus text · a card in a card · a centered hero over three columns · a three-card grid · the hero-metric template · perfectly even spacing · a needless modal · a broadsheet of hairlines, zero radius and dense columns on a subject that is not news.
- COLOR: the AI beige (a cream ground near `#F4F1EA`, a serif display, a terracotta accent near `#D97757`) · a near-black ground with one acid-green or vermilion accent · a purple-to-blue gradient · gradient text · more than one accent per view · color carrying a state alone · light or dark picked by category instead of use scene.
- TYPE: an italic serif display standing in for "elegant" · every heading at nearly the same size · display type at default tracking · monospace as a costume for "technical" · a system face as the voice of a piece that owns a world · an em dash as a general connector · one word of a headline set in italic, bold or another color · three or more sections landing on "X. No Y."
- SHAPE AND SURFACE: one radius on everything · a soft shadow as decoration · a zero-offset halo · a block shadow outside real neobrutalism · a glow on a primary action · a colored side edge above 1px · a tinted ring around a picture · glass and blur as decoration · sparklines and rings standing in for content · a geometric mask faking a photographic edge.
- ICON AND MOTION: an emoji as an icon · mixed icon treatments · a pulsing dot · a spinner where a skeleton belongs · motion on everything · a `→` on every link and button · meta strings joined by middle dots.

THREE THAT ARE HIS, NOT TELLS: the eyebrow above a heading (his deck shell uses one, it carries the section), section numbers 01 / 02 / 03 on his block dividers, and a dark scrim over a picture that carries text. Any public rule that collides with a rule he stated loses, and the reply says so.

THE ACCESSIBILITY HALF: RULE 1's numbers, plus on anything interactive a visible focus state, touch targets of at least 44 by 44, reduced-motion respected, text that reflows at narrow widths, a visible label on every field (a placeholder alone is not a label), and each error directly below its field.

Report it: each tell found, decided or default, and what replaced it.

## RULE 12 — TRIM THE FAT. Less, but better.

On every design, only what matters goes on screen (27 Sep 2026). If an element can go without changing the meaning, it goes.

Dieter Rams, principle 10: good design is as little design as possible. His motto "Weniger, aber besser" means less, but better: keep the essential, drop every non-essential. Tufte says the same for charts: erase every mark that carries no data.

THE TEST, run on every element before handoff: remove it in your head. The meaning stays the same? It goes. The meaning breaks? It stays.

- ON-SCREEN TEXT IS THE FIRST SUSPECT. Keep only the words the picture fails without: a figure, a status, one label that names the beat. When a voice-over or a caption already says it, the frame does not say it again.
- CUT BY DEFAULT: sub-lines under a title, an eyebrow that repeats its title, product names next to a packshot, placeholder rows ("Est. impact +00%"), a label that repeats the VO, a unit the picture already makes clear, a symbol that adds nothing ("=", "×"). His deck-shell eyebrow that names a section stays (RULE 11).
- CHARTS CARRY NO TEXT unless the number is the point: no axis numbers, legends, tick labels or gridlines. The mark alone carries the shape of the data.
- SHORTEN WHAT STAYS: a chip or label says it in one or two words ("Diagnoses", not "It diagnoses the problem").
- The client's words and his words stay. A client figure or line he asked for is never trimmed without asking.
- LAST LOOK: before handoff, remove one accessory (Coco Chanel's rule).
- REPORT IT: list what was cut, one line each, so he can ask for any piece back.
