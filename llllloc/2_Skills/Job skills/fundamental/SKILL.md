# /fundamental — his own art knowledge

Opens his art notes. Runs only when he types its name. No other skill may call it and the router may not pick it. His notes beat an article from the internet: when he tags this skill he wants the answer built on his own thinking about color, light, composition, perspective, drawing or process.

## HARD STOPS

- Never write into `llllloc/6_Fundamental knowledge/` unless his message says to, in that same message.
- Never load this skill on your own.
- Never read more than four notes for one request.

## STEP 1 — OPEN THE BRAIN

Nothing to open: the brain folder is the session's main folder (29 Oct 2026). No notes folder: say so in one line and answer without it.

## STEP 2 — READ THE INDEX, NOT THE LIBRARY

Read one file: `llllloc/6_Fundamental knowledge/FUNDAMENTAL-INDEX.md`. It lists every note in `llllloc/6_Fundamental knowledge/` and what each covers. The folders: `color`, `composition`, `design-thinking`, `drawing`, `graphic-design`, `perspective`, `process`, `technique`, `value-and-light`, `visual-library`, `books-and-pdfs`.

## STEP 3 — PICK TWO TO FOUR NOTES

Match the notes to the tagged question, not to the general topic. Two is the target. Four is the hard cap. A note that only shares a keyword is not a match. Never read a folder whole. The cap is the point of this skill.

## STEP 4 — ANSWER FROM THEM

Build the answer on what the notes say. Use his own wording when it is sharper. When a note and an outside source disagree, his note wins. Say which, in one line. When the notes do not cover the question, say so. Never fill the gap with a guess.

## STEP 5 — THE RECEIPT

End the reply with one line naming the exact files you opened: `Notes: llllloc/6_Fundamental knowledge/color/<file>.md, llllloc/6_Fundamental knowledge/value-and-light/<file>.md`. No line means this skill did not run. Never name a note you did not open.
